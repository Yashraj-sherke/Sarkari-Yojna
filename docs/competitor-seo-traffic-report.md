# Sarkari Yojana Setu: Competitor SEO and Traffic Growth Report

**Prepared:** 30 September 2026  
**Competitors studied:** SarkariYojana.com and myScheme  
**Purpose:** Identify observable traffic and ranking tactics, separate evidence from inference, and convert useful tactics into a safe implementation plan for Sarkari Yojana Setu.

## 1. Executive conclusion

The competitors do not appear to depend on one SEO trick. Their visibility is better explained by a system:

1. They cover thousands of specific user intents around schemes, states, audiences, benefits, forms, eligibility, status, lists and official portals.
2. They use predictable page structures, so users and search engines can quickly locate an answer.
3. They connect detail pages to category, state and supporting pages.
4. They have accumulated a large searchable inventory over time.
5. myScheme adds government ownership, standardized sourcing and personalized discovery, which create strong trust signals.
6. SarkariYojana.com targets a wider range of long-tail phrases in titles, archives, summaries and downloadable lists.

No public inspection can prove the exact percentage of traffic generated by keywords, backlinks, brand searches, Discover, referrals or direct visits. Exact traffic and conversion data require access to the competitor's analytics and Search Console. This report labels public observations as **Observed**, reasonable conclusions as **Inference**, and our proposed work as **Action**.

The correct strategy for Sarkari Yojana Setu is to combine:

- myScheme's trust, structured facts and task-oriented experience;
- SarkariYojana.com's breadth of query coverage and state/category discovery;
- stricter source verification than typical private scheme sites;
- a focused internal-link architecture;
- Search Console data for deciding what to improve next.

## 2. What can and cannot be concluded about competitor traffic

### Publicly observable

- Indexed page topics and URL patterns
- Page titles, headings and content sections
- Navigation and archive structure
- Internal links visible in rendered pages
- Government-source links
- Page templates and search/discovery features
- Public backlink estimates from third-party tools, if supplied
- Search-result visibility for manually checked queries

### Not publicly verifiable without private access

- Exact organic traffic
- Exact keyword conversion rate
- Bounce or engagement rate
- Backlinks that actually influence a specific ranking
- Search Console impressions and click-through rate
- Revenue and ad performance
- Whether a single design element caused ranking improvement

Third-party traffic tools provide estimates, not ground truth. They are useful for prioritization but should not be reported as exact visitor counts.

## 3. Competitor model: SarkariYojana.com

### 3.1 Broad search-intent coverage

**Observed:** Its public titles and scheme lists repeatedly cover action modifiers such as:

- online application form
- apply online
- registration
- eligibility
- documents required
- application status
- beneficiary list
- payment status
- PDF download
- official website
- helpline
- state name

**Inference:** A single scheme can become visible for many lower-volume searches. The combined traffic from many long-tail queries can be substantial even when each query has modest volume.

**Action:** Build one authoritative scheme page for closely related intents and a separate supporting guide only when the intent is distinct enough to deserve a standalone answer.

### 3.2 Large state and category archives

**Observed:** State scheme lists group many scheme pages under a geographic topic. Downloadable state lists also repeat scheme names and common actions such as application, eligibility and status.

**Inference:** These archive pages can rank for broad discovery queries such as “Madhya Pradesh government schemes” and also give crawlers paths to detail pages.

**Action:** Maintain useful, indexable state/category hubs only when they contain reviewed schemes, an original introduction, filters and direct links. Do not index empty or near-duplicate combinations.

### 3.3 Fresh-query targeting

**Observed:** The site uses years, “latest,” installments, lists and updates in content where current events or recurring scheme actions are involved.

**Risk:** Old pages can look current when only the title or year changes. Some publicly visible historical list content contains time-sensitive statements that need fresh official verification.

**Action:** Target a fresh query only after an official notice exists. Update the facts, source, verification date and affected sections. Never roll the title year forward automatically.

### 3.4 Mixed Hindi-English search language

**Observed:** Titles commonly combine Hindi scheme terms with English action words.

**Inference:** This matches how Indian users often search: “yojana online apply,” “status check,” “documents,” and “list.”

**Action:** Use natural mixed terminology where users expect it, while keeping the sentence readable. Avoid exact-match repetition.

### 3.5 Search-result-oriented titles

**Observed:** Titles frequently state the scheme, action and location.

**Benefit:** Users can decide quickly whether the result matches their task.

**Risk:** Overloaded titles can be rewritten by Google and look spammy.

**Action:** Select one main intent per title. Cover secondary intents in headings and page text.

Example:

> PM-KISAN योजना: पात्रता, लाभ, e-KYC और आवेदन प्रक्रिया

Avoid:

> PM Kisan 2026 Apply Online Registration Status List Payment eKYC New Update

### 3.6 Content inventory and age

**Inference:** A long-running domain with a large inventory may have accumulated branded searches, citations, links and crawl history. These cannot be reproduced instantly by changing a layout.

**Action:** Build durable assets consistently: verified detail pages, state/category hubs, problem-solving guides and useful tools. Measure progress over months rather than promising ranking in 24 hours.

## 4. Competitor model: myScheme

### 4.1 Personalized scheme discovery

**Observed:** myScheme lets users enter demographic information and discover potentially relevant schemes. The public workflow is “Enter Details → Search → Select & Apply.”

**Likely value:** It satisfies a strong user need: “Which scheme is suitable for me?” This creates utility beyond publishing articles.

**Action:** Improve `/mere-liye` as a privacy-conscious discovery tool. Ask only the minimum non-sensitive questions needed for filtering. Explain that results are informational and final eligibility is decided by the responsible department.

### 4.2 Standardized detail-page template

**Observed:** Scheme pages consistently expose sections such as:

- details
- benefits
- eligibility
- exclusions
- application process
- documents required
- frequently asked questions
- sources and references

**Likely value:** Consistency helps users scan unfamiliar schemes and helps search engines identify the page's main entities and answers.

**Action:** Enforce the same core content contract in the Sarkari Yojana Setu data schema. Do not index an active page until required verified fields pass review.

### 4.3 Precise process detail

**Observed:** Some pages include upload formats, size limits, office names, acknowledgement steps and tracking instructions.

**Likely value:** This information completes the user's task and covers highly specific searches naturally.

**Action:** Capture these details only when official instructions support them. Practical specificity is more valuable than generic word count.

### 4.4 Official-source pathways

**Observed:** myScheme directs users to the concerned ministry or department's application destination and includes sources or references.

**Action:** Display descriptive official-source links, department, accessed date and the claim supported. Do not make an unofficial link look like an application button.

### 4.5 Government trust advantage

**Observed:** myScheme identifies Digital India Corporation, MeitY and Government of India ownership.

**Inference:** Government ownership creates trust and brand demand that an independent website cannot copy.

**Action:** Compete through transparency: clearly identify Sarkari Yojana Setu as independent, show editorial methodology, named reviewer where possible, dated sources, correction channel and security guidance.

### 4.6 Category, state and ministry discovery

**Observed:** The platform supports discovery by categories, states/UTs and central ministries.

**Action:** Make these entities first-class navigation paths. Index a hub only if it provides real choice and original value; keep empty or mechanically generated filter pages out of the index.

## 5. How long-tail traffic compounds

A broad query such as `PM Kisan` is competitive and ambiguous. A user progresses through several intents:

1. Discovery: `PM Kisan yojana kya hai`
2. Qualification: `PM Kisan eligibility land limit`
3. Preparation: `PM Kisan documents required`
4. Action: `PM Kisan registration kaise kare`
5. Verification: `PM Kisan eKYC kaise kare`
6. Tracking: `PM Kisan beneficiary status check`
7. Troubleshooting: `PM Kisan payment pending reason`
8. Navigation: `PM Kisan official website`

One authoritative page can answer the common closely related intents. A detailed e-KYC or payment troubleshooting guide can be separate when it offers a complete standalone solution. Together they form a topic cluster.

The traffic opportunity is the sum of many useful answers. Repeating a keyword is not the mechanism.

## 6. Query-to-page decision framework

| Query type | Preferred destination | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Scheme definition | Main scheme page | PM-KISAN क्या है |
| Core benefit | Main scheme page | PM-KISAN में कितनी राशि मिलती है |
| Eligibility | Main scheme page | PM-KISAN पात्रता |
| Basic application | Main scheme page | PM-KISAN registration |
| Detailed technical task | Supporting guide if substantial | PM-KISAN e-KYC biometric process |
| Troubleshooting | Supporting guide if substantial | OTP नहीं आ रहा |
| Geographic discovery | State hub | MP सरकारी योजनाएं |
| Audience discovery | Category/audience hub | महिलाओं के लिए सरकारी योजनाएं |
| Time-sensitive official event | Verified update | Official installment announcement |
| Pure filter combination with no unique value | Do not index | state + category + arbitrary sort |

Before creating a URL, ask:

1. Is the intent different from an existing page?
2. Can the page provide a complete standalone answer?
3. Is the information officially verifiable?
4. Will another page compete for the same query?
5. Can users and crawlers reach it through a relevant internal link?

If the answer to the first two questions is no, improve an existing page instead.

## 7. On-page techniques worth adopting

### 7.1 Direct answer first

Put a 40–80 word factual answer below the H1. State what the scheme is, who it serves, the main verified benefit and current status when known.

### 7.2 One clear H1

Use one prominent H1 that matches the page's main task. Google may use headings and other prominent text when forming title links.

### 7.3 Quick facts

Use a short table for scheme name, department, beneficiary, benefit, application method, status, official destination and last verification.

### 7.4 Question navigation

Use anchor links such as:

- कौन पात्र है?
- कितना लाभ मिलेगा?
- कौन से दस्तावेज चाहिए?
- आवेदन कैसे करें?
- स्थिति कैसे देखें?
- समस्या होने पर क्या करें?

### 7.5 Eligibility and exclusions

Separate positive conditions from disqualifying conditions. Users often need both to decide whether to continue.

### 7.6 Exact process steps

Name the portal, buttons, required identifiers, upload limits and acknowledgement process when verified. Avoid generic “visit website and apply” text.

### 7.7 Problem-solving section

Answer real post-application problems. These are useful long-tail queries and increase the chance that the user completes the task without returning to search.

### 7.8 Visible sources

Show official source title, department, direct link, update date when known, accessed date and what it supports.

### 7.9 Update history

Record meaningful changes. Do not refresh the visible date when facts did not change.

### 7.10 Natural Hindi-English vocabulary

Use terms such as Online Apply, Registration, Status Check, e-KYC and Official Website where natural. Do not paste a keyword list into paragraphs.

## 8. Titles and snippets

Google says title links may be derived from the `<title>`, H1, headings, prominent text and link anchors. Titles should be unique, concise and accurate.

Recommended pattern:

> [Scheme]: [primary user task] और [one or two important verified subtopics]

Examples:

- PM-KISAN योजना: पात्रता, लाभ और e-KYC प्रक्रिया
- लाड़ली बहना योजना: पात्रता, आवेदन और भुगतान स्थिति
- आयुष्मान भारत कार्ड: पात्रता और आवेदन प्रक्रिया

Google primarily creates snippets from visible page content and may use the meta description when it describes the page better. Therefore:

- write a unique meta description;
- ensure the first paragraphs contain the best direct answer;
- do not fill the meta description with keywords;
- include useful page-specific details.

## 9. Internal-link system

Recommended hierarchy:

```text
Homepage
├── Audience/category hubs
│   ├── Scheme pages
│   │   ├── Process guides
│   │   └── Troubleshooting guides
└── State hubs
    └── Scheme pages
```

Rules:

1. Every important indexable page receives at least one internal link.
2. Use actual crawlable links with an `href`.
3. Use descriptive anchor text.
4. Link in context, close to the relevant explanation.
5. Link from guides back to the parent scheme.
6. Link from scheme pages to the applicable category/state hub.
7. Do not auto-link every mention of a word.
8. Do not add unrelated links only to increase link count.
9. Check for orphan pages and broken links in CI.
10. Keep pagination crawlable and canonicalized correctly.

Good anchor:

> PM-KISAN e-KYC की आधिकारिक प्रक्रिया

Weak anchor:

> यहां क्लिक करें

## 10. Category and state hubs

A useful hub should contain:

- a clear H1;
- a short original explanation;
- the audience or location covered;
- reviewed scheme listings;
- filters that do not create uncontrolled indexable URLs;
- links to high-value schemes;
- guidance for selecting a scheme;
- pagination when needed;
- a canonical URL;
- an empty-state policy.

Do not index a hub merely because a state/category combination can be generated. A hub with no reviewed records should be `noindex` or absent.

## 11. Technical SEO foundations

### 11.1 Crawlability

- Essential text must be present in rendered HTML.
- Important links must be crawlable.
- robots.txt must not block scheme pages or required resources.
- Pages intended for search must return HTTP 200.

### 11.2 Index control

- Index reviewed, useful and canonical pages.
- `noindex` drafts, admin pages, saved/reminder pages, private tools and weak search results.
- Avoid soft 404 pages that return 200 for missing schemes.

### 11.3 Canonicals

- Give each content item one preferred URL.
- Self-canonicalize preferred pages.
- Redirect real duplicates where appropriate.
- Include canonical URLs, not aliases, in the sitemap.

### 11.4 Sitemap

- Include only canonical indexable URLs.
- Use absolute URLs.
- Use `lastmod` only when it reflects a real content change.
- Submit the sitemap in Search Console.
- Separate sitemaps by content type later if the inventory grows enough to aid monitoring.

### 11.5 Structured data

- Use BreadcrumbList for visible breadcrumbs.
- Use WebPage or Article only when accurate.
- Use FAQPage only for visible qualifying questions and answers.
- Use GovernmentService only when the page accurately represents the service and verified properties.
- Do not claim that the independent website is a government provider.
- Do not add fake ratings or hidden schema content.
- Passing a schema validator does not guarantee a rich result.

### 11.6 Performance

- Prefer text and CSS for essential page structure.
- Use responsive images with explicit dimensions.
- Lazy-load non-critical images.
- Avoid a large grid of image-heavy cards above the fold.
- Keep JavaScript out of content that does not require interaction.
- Monitor Core Web Vitals with field data, not only local scores.

### 11.7 Mobile usability

- Keep primary answers visible without expanding accordions.
- Stack tables or allow safe horizontal scrolling.
- Use large touch targets.
- Avoid sticky elements that hide content.
- Test on slow mobile connections.

## 12. Trust and editorial techniques

Government-benefit content affects financial and administrative decisions. Trust should be visible:

- independent-site disclosure;
- named author or reviewer where possible;
- editorial policy;
- correction policy;
- official source links;
- last verified date;
- next review date internally;
- distinction between confirmed, closed, draft and verification-required content;
- warnings against sharing OTP, PIN or passwords;
- clear indication when application happens outside the site.

Do not imitate government branding, emblems or language in a way that suggests official ownership.

## 13. Backlinks and authority

Competitor authority may partly reflect links and mentions accumulated over time, but a public page inspection cannot quantify their causal value.

Safe link-earning assets for Sarkari Yojana Setu:

- carefully verified state scheme directories;
- original comparison tables based on official rules;
- downloadable checklists generated from verified sources;
- change logs for important schemes;
- official-link directories;
- accessible Hindi explanations of difficult notifications;
- embeddable, cited data summaries;
- correction-friendly source pages that journalists and community organizations can reference.

Promotion options:

- share useful resources with relevant community groups;
- notify organizations only when a resource genuinely helps their audience;
- publish original data or analysis that earns citations;
- maintain accurate organization profiles;
- make pages easy to cite with stable URLs and dates.

Avoid:

- buying followed links;
- mass directory submissions;
- comment spam;
- automated guest-post networks;
- exchanging links at scale;
- fake scholarship or application pages;
- exact-match anchor campaigns.

Google defines links created primarily to manipulate rankings as link spam.

## 14. Content operations and query pipeline

### Weekly workflow

1. Export Search Console queries by page.
2. Identify queries with impressions and positions 8–30.
3. Group variants by intent.
4. Check whether the correct page answers the intent.
5. Verify the answer with the responsible government source.
6. Improve the existing page before creating a new URL.
7. Add a guide only for a distinct, substantial intent.
8. Add relevant internal links.
9. Request indexing for a small number of important changes if appropriate.
10. Record the change and compare performance after enough data accumulates.

### Query action matrix

| Signal | Likely issue | Action |
|---|---|---|
| High impressions, low CTR | Result presentation mismatch | Improve title, description and direct answer |
| Position 8–20 | Relevance or completeness gap | Add verified answer, sources and internal links |
| Multiple pages alternate in results | Cannibalization | Merge intent or strengthen canonical page |
| Crawled, not indexed | Weak/duplicate value or quality issue | Improve uniqueness and internal linking; reconsider indexing |
| Discovered, not crawled | Crawl priority/architecture | Add meaningful links and clean sitemap |
| Traffic drops after facts age | Stale information | Reverify official sources and update materially |
| Query has no verified answer | Publication risk | Do not speculate; document uncertainty |

### Editorial queue fields

- query
- intent cluster
- destination page
- impressions
- clicks
- CTR
- average position
- official source
- source date
- verification date
- responsible editor
- change made
- review outcome
- next review date

## 16. Current gaps and risks found in the repository

### Priority 0: Evidence quality at scale

The Madhya Pradesh dataset contains a large number of records sharing the same verification date. A shared date is not automatically wrong, but it makes review provenance difficult to assess. Each record should have dated official references and claim-level support adequate for its published fields.

### Priority 0: GovernmentService test disagreement

The scheme detail route emits GovernmentService structured data, while an existing SEO test expects sitemap pages not to use that type. Decide the policy, then align implementation and tests. Do not keep a schema merely because a competitor uses rich markup.

### Priority 1: Sub-intent URL control

The repository contains a dynamic `/yojna/[slug]/[subIntent]` route. Audit it carefully for thin or duplicate pages. Only allow a finite set of distinct, content-rich intents. Keep generated variants out of the sitemap until they pass the same editorial checks as main scheme pages.

### Priority 1: State coverage

The sitemap currently adds a Madhya Pradesh state hub conditionally. Expand state hubs only as verified inventory becomes substantial. Do not generate all states with sparse content.

### Priority 1: News/update workflow

The repository correctly notes that the generator has no measured trend feed or official-announcement retrieval. Keep generated news as unverified drafts. Require a recorded Search Console/manual query, official notice and human review before indexation.

### Priority 1: Tracking guidance completeness

The health check already flags missing tracking guidance. Use it as a publishing gate when status tracking is a common user need; record that no official online tracking exists when that is the verified reality.

### Priority 2: Guides and query clusters

Current guides are limited. Expand only from demonstrated user needs: e-KYC, status interpretation, application correction, document preparation and fraud avoidance. Each guide must connect back to applicable schemes.

### Priority 2: Search Console integration

The strongest next step is not guessing more keywords. Build a recurring report that maps Search Console queries to pages, flags positions 8–30, and detects cannibalization. Human review should decide changes.

## 17. Recommended 90-day plan

### Days 1–15: Quality and measurement

- Connect and verify Search Console and analytics.
- Submit and validate the sitemap.
- Establish dashboards for clicks, impressions, CTR, position, indexed pages and Core Web Vitals.
- Resolve the GovernmentService schema/test policy.
- Audit dynamic sub-intent pages.
- Select 20 highest-value verified schemes.
- Confirm official sources and freshness for those pages.

### Days 16–30: Page quality

- Apply the standard scheme content contract to the selected pages.
- Add direct answers, quick facts, eligibility, exclusions, exact process, troubleshooting and sources.
- Improve unique titles and descriptions.
- Add descriptive internal links from hubs and guides.
- Remove unsupported dates and claims.

### Days 31–60: Topic clusters

- Use Search Console to identify distinct long-tail gaps.
- Publish a small number of deep supporting guides.
- Improve category and Madhya Pradesh hubs.
- Add orphan-page and broken-link checks.
- Add content freshness and overdue-review reporting.

### Days 61–90: Authority and iteration

- Publish one or two original, citable resources.
- Promote them to genuinely relevant organizations.
- Compare query groups before and after changes.
- Consolidate pages with overlapping intent.
- Expand to another state only after the review workflow is reliable.

## 18. Measurement framework

Track separately by page type:

- scheme detail pages;
- state hubs;
- category hubs;
- guides;
- updates.

Core metrics:

- valid indexed URLs;
- impressions;
- clicks;
- CTR;
- median position by query cluster;
- non-brand clicks;
- number of queries in positions 1–3, 4–10, 11–20 and 21–50;
- pages with no internal links;
- pages missing official references;
- pages past next review date;
- broken internal and official links;
- Core Web Vitals pass rate;
- successful outbound clicks to official portals, measured without collecting sensitive application data.

Use 28-day and 90-day comparisons. Avoid judging a page from one day of rankings.

## 19. Small techniques that matter

- Keep scheme names spelled consistently.
- Record aliases in search data, not as repetitive prose.
- Use one preferred slug.
- Link abbreviations to the full scheme name in the introduction.
- Include state names only when applicable.
- Distinguish a loan, subsidy, grant, pension and insurance benefit accurately.
- State whether an amount is monthly, annual, one-time or reimbursement-based.
- Separate applicant documents from institution documents.
- State whether copies must be self-attested when verified.
- State file formats and size limits when official instructions provide them.
- Tell users to save acknowledgement numbers.
- Identify whether status requires login, Aadhaar, mobile number or application ID.
- Explain common status labels only from official evidence.
- Make external government links visually clear.
- Open external links consistently without trapping keyboard users.
- Never collect Aadhaar, OTP or banking secrets on an information page.
- Provide a correction link near sourced content.
- Use real content dates in the sitemap.
- Return a real 404 for unknown scheme slugs.
- Avoid indexing internal search-result combinations.
- Keep pagination links crawlable.
- Avoid duplicate H1 and title templates.
- Ensure images have dimensions and meaningful alt text when informative.
- Use empty alt text for decorative images.
- Keep the main answer available without JavaScript interaction.
- Do not hide all useful content inside accordions.
- Test Hindi font rendering and line height on mobile.
- Preserve URL stability when titles change.
- Redirect renamed slugs permanently.
- Update internal links to the final URL instead of relying on redirects.
- Recheck official links after department sites migrate.
- Keep old closed schemes available only when the historical page remains useful and clearly labelled.
- Do not add “latest” to evergreen content.
- Do not publish a “last date” section when no deadline is confirmed.
- Do not publish fabricated popularity labels such as “most applied.”
- Use FAQs for real questions, not keyword variations.
- Do not assume FAQ markup will create a rich result.
- Keep author and reviewer information truthful.
- Avoid intrusive ads around application buttons.
- Distinguish our navigation button from the official application button.

## 20. Techniques to reject even if competitors appear to use them

- Keyword stuffing
- Changing only the year in old titles
- Mass-producing near-identical state pages
- Scraping and paraphrasing official or competitor content without added value
- Publishing unverified installment dates
- Creating fake application functionality
- Hiding text for crawlers
- Buying links
- Misrepresenting government affiliation
- Using structured data that is not visible or accurate
- Creating many pages only to capture spelling variants
- Publishing automated trending news without measured demand and official evidence

These practices can create short-term impressions but introduce user harm and Google spam-policy risk.

## 21. Recommended operating principle

Use this rule for every change:

> One scheme, one authoritative canonical page; one genuinely distinct problem, one supporting guide; every important claim tied to an official source; every important page reachable through useful internal links; every new page justified by user need.

## 23. Final assessment

The competitor advantage is mainly a content-and-discovery system built over time: broad query coverage, stable information architecture, many internally connected pages, recognizable brands and task-oriented page templates. It is not evidence that every competitor tactic is safe or that copying their keywords will reproduce their traffic.

Sarkari Yojana Setu already has stronger-than-basic foundations for verification, indexing control, sitemap generation and health checks. Its next growth constraint is operational: consistently converting measured user questions into thoroughly verified answers, then connecting those answers through focused hubs and guides. Improving this workflow is more defensible and sustainable than rapidly generating more pages.
